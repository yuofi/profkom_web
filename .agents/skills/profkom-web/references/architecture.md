# Architecture and domain invariants

Use the current source/tests when a recorded detail disagrees. This repository is the live project; sibling export/archive copies are out of scope.

## Runtime and ownership

The primary checkout owns the active code. Local runtimes, verification launchers,
logs, transfer artifacts and preserved worktree files may live in ignored `.local/`.
The local launcher derives its paths from this checkout; historical auxiliary
scripts are retained as evidence and are not the default development entry point.


React/Vite -> nginx -> same-origin `/api` -> FastAPI -> SQLite and contextual S3 I/O. FastAPI creates one `/api` router in main.py; domain modules register their routes on it. The HereBot service router `/api/bot/v1` (bot_api.py) is separate: a token check without the database and the ordinary unit of work, no JWT or working-access guard. OpenAPI remains `/openapi.json`.

- database.py owns legacy/core ORM, additive import-time schema upgrades, conversions, synchronization and Database.atomic. domain_models.py registers normalized extension tables on the same Base before create_all.
- models.py contains internal dataclasses; main.py and domain_contract.py define HTTP schemas. New domain services may return validated dictionaries rather than legacy dataclasses.
- access.py holds the release 1 access policy: `POLICY` maps each action to the relations that allow it (a rule may narrow by season state), `relations()` derives the actor's relations to a block, event, subevent or material, and `allowed()` backs both server checks and `capabilities`. Add or change a rule there, not at a call site; 403 messages stay at the call sites. registration, membership, participation, season_summaries, season_corrections, report_access, materials, directory, accounts and workspace own their corresponding workflows.
- auth.py owns passwords, JWT/session versions, refresh rotation and the central approval guard. vk_auth.py verifies provider data and explicit external identities; it never downloads a VK photo.
- file_access.py owns contextual uploads, actual-object verification and current ACL. Uploads target private/file_id staging; complete verifies a bounded GET snapshot and conditionally creates sealed/file_id with IfNoneMatch=*. Completed downloads/avatars use only sealed keys. Before cutover, batch-seal old completed IDs with scripts/seal_completed_files.py while the old API is stopped; its default dry-run is read-only and does not import settings or call storage. Lazy sealing on first read remains a compatibility fallback without a DB schema change; retention queues both keys. Provider support for conditional server-side PutObject is a deployment check. legacy_files.py resolves saved configured-bucket references at read time.

## Authorization and identity

All working content requires an active approved account, including blocks, legacy guides, files and contacts. Public auth operations do not expose content. Pending/rejected users have their own registration/session workflow. Backend checks remain authoritative even when UI buttons are hidden. With the HereBot integration on (BOT_API.md, stage 2) the VK link through the bot is part of registration: an application is approvable only when complete and linked with state `ok`, and from `BOT_LINK_DEADLINE` guard_working_access (and `recheck_current_user(working=True)`) refuses an active account without it with 403 `bot_link_required`, after the temporary-password check; `/bot-link/*` and `/bot/console/*` stay open. The state is copied to `users.bot_link_state` by `bot_api.save_link` in the same transaction as `bot_links`, so the gate and `/api/session` read no extra SQL.

Chair/deputies come from explicit leadership/role assignments; the deputies share the chair's rights except opening and closing seasons and leadership changes; a closed season is changed only by the chair, the deputies and the HR master (owner 2026-10-31). Block master, per-principal deputies, event/subevent leaders and organizers grant scoped rights; event leaders and deputies also lead their subevents. The master of the explicitly configured is_hr block has global HR/KKR rights. Its actual deputy can read all block applications, decide the HR stage and, by the 2026-10-30 owner decision, has global KKR/summary/analytics and public task-edit rights; in the HR block itself they are an ordinary block deputy (GL-09, 2026-10-31). This does not grant report, event-management, former-contact or participant-category authority. Material editors are assigned per material by the chair and deputies; a block guide is also edited by the block's master and deputy. PGAS is edited by the chair, deputies and pgas_admin. The HereBot console belongs to the master of the block named exactly `IT` and the bot maintainers they assign (not the chair or deputies); broadcasts to the chair, deputies and the senders they assign. Those grants are `role_assignments` rows with `scope_kind = "site"` and `scope_id = null` (owner, 2026-11-04). A command on an object its caller cannot read (task, assignment, comment, report, material, upload owner, block request) answers 404 like its GET, before the revision or the command right is compared; a reader without the right still gets 403.

The service (root) account (backend/docs/SERVICE_ACCOUNT.md, owner 2026-11-08) is one `users` row with `is_service = 1` (partial unique index), made, updated or disabled only by the idempotent startup step `service_account.sync()` from `ROOT_LOGIN`/`ROOT_PASSWORD_HASH` in backend/.env; without either it cannot sign in and its tokens answer 401. It signs in through `POST /auth/login` with the exact login in `email`, per-client failure limit 429, and every sign-in and every third failure in a row notifies the chair and the master of the block named exactly `IT` in the bell only. `access.relations` gives it every relation (`SERVICE_RELATIONS`); rights paths outside `access.allowed` check `service_account.is_service`. It is never a person: every list or count of people filters with the one helper `service_account.visible()` (`visible_ids` for ids in hand), choosing it as assignee, member, role holder, recipient or grantee answers 422 `unknown_user` (`service_account.refuse`) or 404, and it is shown as `UserRef(user_id=None, «Сервисный аккаунт»)`. It has no VK link, banner, deadline gate or VK delivery; its bot update requests (`updates_check`, `update`, `rollback`) answer 409 `bot_console_service_vk` while the owner question is open. Its reserved contact address passes `MeOut` and `ProfileUpdate`, and `update_profile` keeps it to that account alone, unchanged (422 at `email` both ways). People lists already come without it and task join/watch capabilities are not issued to it; where the client itself would offer the account to itself, it reads `service_account: true` from `/session`: no self pick in the task form, no block application, no team/achievement/activity cards or editor on its own profile (those reads answer 403/404 for it), and the console's update buttons disabled with that refusal's text. Never infer its rights from legacy `admin`/`super_user` flags; a new people list must use `visible()`.

Owner access decisions of 2026-10-31 (rules in `access.POLICY`; capabilities follow them): the KKR fill-in summon (`kkr.remind`, season capability `remind_kkr`) belongs only to the HR master and the actual HR deputy, in open or summarizing seasons; the chair and deputies do not summon (SE-14). A closed season — summary marks, corrections, coefficients, public task fields, assessment inputs and dates, reviews, merit edits and new merits (`merit.manage.*`/`merit.create.*` narrow to it there; `POST /merits` only, the UI shows no new-merit form for a closed season) — is changed by `CLOSED_SEASON` = the chair, the deputies and the HR master, not by the HR deputy or area leads; the UI follows `edit_decisions`/`correct_summary`/`edit_coefficient`/`edit_task`, not `correct_season`. SE-13 does not narrow a task reader's controls: in a closed season every reader, the HR deputy included, still changes the public status, submits a result, ticks checklist marks (`toggle_task_checklist`, description marks too) and comments, without touching accounting. A new assignment row in a closed season (roster join/take/assign, reassignment: `task.add_assignment`) is added by the same `CLOSED_SEASON` circle, not the HR deputy (TR-05); season lifecycle commands stay chair-only (SE-13). The actual HR deputy is an ordinary deputy of the HR block (`BLOCK_DEPUTY` there, plus the global `QUEUE_HOLDER`/`BLOCK_LEAD_ANYWHERE`; the old exception and `HR_BLOCK_DEPUTY` are gone): queue creation in that block, guide read/edit/publish/delete, block card editing (description, community link, avatar) and the block deputy's task, roster and assessment rights, beside every global HR-deputy right; block rename stays with the chair and deputies, and no closed-season rule names a block or area lead, so the HR deputy makes no SE-13 closed-season change there either, keeping only the reader controls (GL-09). Only the chair archives or restores a chair deputy, the deputy themselves included (`leadership.deputies`, 403 `leadership_change_forbidden`, refused before restore mapping/master-conflict prompts); deputies keep archive/restore of ordinary accounts, `GET /accounts/archive` items carry `restore_account` per account and the directory carries `archive_account` per target (PE-01). A planned season takes no marks: `summary.decide` is empty there, the draft summary carries `edit_decisions` only when the rule allows it for the reader and season state, and PUT …/summary/decisions answers 403; its summary, xlsx, coefficients and accounting reads stay (SE-21).

GET requests only read. Block identities, legacy roster memberships, one guide placeholder per block, old-guide material mappings and registration applications are made by their writers (create_block, roster writers, guide deletion) and repaired once by the idempotent startup preset (scripts/preset_db.py); a block without an identity row is read with its derived ID, unsaved. test_get_without_read_repair.py records the statements of these GETs.

Never infer rights from FIO, kkr_name, legacy admin/super_user flags or events_roles. A saved trusted master_user_id or explicit operator-ID mapping is different from matching a display name. VK subject links are explicit; provider email or FIO cannot authenticate an existing local account. Password change, archive and logout-all revoke existing access/refresh sessions. Role changes are checked from current DB state on every request. Session access_revision also fingerprints ordinary block membership and subevent organizer rosters; the frontend removes private cache and remounts its observers when access changes. Session is checked on focus and every 60 seconds.

## Transactions, IDs and history

Every HTTP command uses one unit of work. The async HTTP dependency uses Database.async_atomic: writers await a local lock and SQLite BEGIN/commit/rollback run outside the event loop; synchronous services/scripts retain Database.atomic. Keep ContextVar propagation and rollback intact. Nested Database operations flush instead of independently committing. SQLite BEGIN IMMEDIATE serializes writes; revisions and unique indexes cover competing planned/current seasons, roles, contributions, final reports, summaries and memberships. Invalid bulk commands roll back completely. Do not bypass synchronization helpers with ad-hoc roster writes. SQLite WAL with synchronous=FULL is opt-in (PB-31, owner 2026-10-31): with SQLITE_JOURNAL_MODE=WAL database._sqlite_journal sets both on every new connection through the DBAPI cursor, never exec_driver_sql, which would end read_context's memo. Without it (the default) the database keeps or returns to the rollback journal, so a deploy never turns WAL on by itself: a server switches in its own owner-approved step (DEPLOY.md, section 7). The backend suite runs in WAL (conftest). The engine is disposed on application shutdown (uvicorn re-raises SIGTERM and skips atexit) and at the exit of scripts, so a stopped process leaves only the database file. Readers and the single writer no longer wait for each other; acknowledged commits stay durable. `-wal`/`-shm` are part of the database: copy it only with the backup API and never delete them while it is in use (DEPLOY.md, section 7).

Block IDs are stable UUIDs mapped to legacy names in BlockMeta; internal legacy task/role/source storage still uses names in some tables, so use membership boundary conversion helpers. Renaming must preserve IDs and update coupled internal names. Master/deputy service roles are independent from ordinary membership.

Registration applications have a bounded resubmission history. Block requests have separate HR/block stages and server-owned 72-hour clocks. Membership and active/approved participation intervals drive category promotion; scores do not imply tenure. Unknown imported dates remain unknown. A separate observed-state migration records participation from its observation timestamp onward, enabling future tenure without backdating a legacy join.

Closed summary versions freeze participants, categories, master recipients, bonus bases and the previous-summary version. Corrections are atomic and versioned. Report publication/version history is independent of KKR. Closed report editors are saved actual leaders/deputies; removed authors get no fallback right. Current corresponding-series leadership grants published historical read, not draft/edit or past tracker access.

KKR criteria come from one table on each side: `database.KKR_CRITERIA_TABLE` (key, owner wording, multiplier; formula, defaults, readers, XLSX, history) and `frontend/src/utils/kkrCriteria.ts` (labels/order), compared by `tests/test_kkr_criteria.py`. Stored criteria JSON may lack keys added later (urgent, monotonous, physical: 2026-11-08); every reader fills them as false via `criteria_flags`, so old scores and revisions stay, and input schemas default them. A new criterion needs the table, the `Criteria` dataclass, `CriteriaSchema`, `TemplateCriteria`, the TS `Criteria`/`emptyCriteria` and the labels.

Season analytics is a read-only global summary/KKR projection. Distinct privately accepted evaluated tasks and accepted-assignment hours remain separate from all-contribution KKR, including negatives/merits/bonuses. Weeks use actual work dates in Moscow time, with unknown dates separately unallocated; this is no reconstructed historical balance. Closed seasons use the latest approved immutable snapshot. New versions embed internal analytics facts (stripped from HTTP summary DTOs); older versions are never backfilled and retain null task/hour/date gaps. Actual HR deputies share global summary/KKR analytics authority. See backend/docs/SEASON_ANALYTICS.md.

Materials separate shared draft and published content. Legacy guide IDs/texts and PGAS references survive explicit adapters. Current contact writes are owner-only; former cards are independent records edited by the chair, the deputies and the HR master. Avatars use local presets or a completed owned file. Draft attachments remain private until publication.

Archive preserves inactive assignments, closes intervals and revokes sessions. Restore resolves occupied master positions and never resurrects global roles from a previous chair term or site-wide bot/broadcast grants. After three calendar years, retention replaces account identity with an anonymous accounting participant, preserving agreed former cards and numeric history. Avatar deletion uses a retry queue after the DB transaction. Internal disabled tombstones preserve historical non-null FKs; do not physically delete an account through HTTP.

## Migration and deployment

Startup migrations are idempotent, but can reject conflicting legacy rows. Rehearse on a backed-up copy using scripts/migrate_v13.py; dry-run must not import the app or write schema. Privileged/VK/HR mappings use verified IDs; never guess historical dates. See backend/docs/MIGRATION_V13.md and ACCOUNT_RETENTION.md.

The backend Docker image runs Python 3.12 in production mode and excludes secret files. Compose injects backend/.env at runtime. Production requires a unique JWT secret; empty-install chair bootstrap uses explicit configured credentials and never resets/elevates an existing account by email. Private bucket ACL/CORS, provider credentials, actual image build and deployment need environment-specific verification.

The personal home reads `/participation`, a compact projection of the actor's actual block memberships and team assignments in non-closed seasons. Global chair/HR rights do not expand this projection; `/dashboard` remains the separate compatible operational projection. Meeting overviews distinguish the event's main announcement and readable child-team announcements; cached meeting data and editors require current owner access.

Frontend uses a React Router data router. UnsavedChangesProvider blocks route/POP exits and beforeunload; local modal/context exits use scoped guards so discarding one editor does not erase unrelated drafts. ActionForm registers fields plus explicit external Markdown/attachment state and upload activity. Navigation saving uses normal validation/revision checks, never automatically publishes or runs destructive commands. Opt-in useForm guards protect standalone PGAS/guide modals; auth forms are excluded. Successful save clears the leave-warning baseline while keeping versioned editor input/revision protected against foreign refetch until explicit replacement.

Frontend uses shared CSS Modules/tokens, TanStack Query, Axios single-flight refresh and focused API modules. API 1.3 types are in utils/api/contract.ts; legacy adapters remain in types.ts. Development demo mode is separate from real-local API integration and disabled in production. Old unscoped upload is deprecated; use filesApi intent/upload/complete. Every refusal of the API carries a JSON `detail` (`{code, message, fields}`, a plain string on the legacy routes of api_contract.LEGACY_ROUTES); messages.ts words it by `code`, then by status. A blob read's refusal body is read back to JSON in api/index.ts, so file previews, the xlsx export and support screenshots keep the code too. A 403 without any detail (ApiError.explained false: a proxy's page, not this API) gets its own text that does not blame a removed role; broadcasts refuse with `broadcast_send_forbidden` / `broadcast_senders_forbidden`.

The HereBot VK link (stage 2, backend/docs/BOT_API.md) reaches the frontend as `session.bot_link` and `RegistrationApplication.bot_link` (null while the integration is off). ## Unified workspace extensions

WEBSUPPORT is a dedicated service queue in an explicitly configured stable block. Its staff gate is checked before generic global-queue readership. An approved reporter reads only the linked own ticket, with ordinary public tracker actions (join, take, submit stay, owner 2026-10-31, GL-22); this grants neither whole-queue access nor private KKR or internal support notes. Sharing a ticket belongs to support staff: a reporter who is not staff is not a task-access manager (`tracker_collaboration._managers`, no `manage_task_access`; approve/reject/revoke answer 403 "Support ticket sharing is managed by support staff"), while the WEBSUPPORT queue owner and a staff member on a ticket they wrote keep it; staff reading someone else's ticket never had it. Anonymous intake is write-only. Feedback persists independently of task creation, uses idempotency and bounded fair retries, and private sanitized diagnostics expire after 90 days. Protected screenshots are fetched through the authenticated API client as blobs.

Profile edits and avatar drafts share one atomic save/revision/fingerprint. Awards retain issue/revoke snapshots; KKR templates retain the selected template and actual applied values in the accounting transaction. Notification preferences collect reference-only delivery events; email and Telegram stay `not_connected` (no provider sender). With the HereBot integration on, the VK channel is the bot's queue (BOT_API.md, stage 3): `pending` rows of `notification_delivery_events` whose recipient's `users.bot_link_state` takes messages; GET /api/bot/v1/work rechecks the account, preference and the bell's own access (workspace.notification_views) per recipient and sends the bell's text with a link built by `bot_api.ROUTES`, the server copy of the frontend `targetRoute` (a test compares their target kinds, so a new target kind needs both). Stage 4 broadcasts (broadcasts.py): `broadcast.send` holders send a text; one write unit fixes the VK recipients (active, not banned, `bot_link_state` sendable, with the link's VK at that moment) by one INSERT … SELECT in batches of 100 by user_id and gives every active account but the author a `broadcast` bell notification through the bulk `notifications.emit_everyone` (no outbox row: `broadcast` has no delivery category). `/work` hands out up to 5 due batches; `/report` applies their results in bulk (bot_api.apply_batches). Any active account reads a broadcast; only senders see counts. Stage 5 console (bot_console.py, `/api/bot/console/*`, `bot.console.view`/`bot.console.operate`): the status (moved there from bot_api.py) adds the pause and the queue counted by the partial pending indexes; PUT /pause flips `bot_status.paused` with a `bot_pause`/`bot_resume` audit only on a change; POST /commands queues one of restart, logs, updates_check, update, rollback in `bot_commands` (one open command per kind, update and rollback share one, a VK link for the update kinds, a restart at most every 2 min, a request key) and never calls the bot. `/work` starts with one query for the pause and whether live commands exist (bot_api.console_flags), reads commands by `ix_bot_commands_open` only then and, while paused, hands out commands only; `/report` applies command results by one read and one executemany UPDATE (bot_api.apply_commands), and the hourly pass expires them (pending 10 min, running 30 min), wipes outputs after 24 h and keeps 30 days and at most 500 rows.
